## **1  Competition format** 

Two teams race simultaneously on separate, mirrored lanes; each robot follows the guide line autonomously. The higher official 100-point score wins each match, subject to the published tie-breakers. Qualification uses a knockout-based format, with wildcard opportunities where announced. 

|**Round**|**Match time limit**|**Purpose**|
|---|---|---|
|1  Basic|3 minutes (180 s)|Initial qualification and any wildcard matches.|
|2  Advanced|3 minutes (180 s)|Knockout championship, including placing matches.|



### **1.1  Basic qualification** 

Teams compete in paired Basic matches. Winners advance to Advanced under the published qualification arrangements. Teams eliminated in the initial stage may be offered a wildcard opportunity, provided they have not been disqualified. Wildcard selection considers official scores, valid completion times and published tie-breakers. 

The Organising Committee will determine qualification places, wildcard places, match pairings and any byes from the total number of entrants. The competition bracket and advancement procedure will be communicated before the competition begins. Necessary format changes must be communicated to all affected teams before implementation.

### **1.3  Seeding and lane allocation** 

Officials seed teams using their official Basic scores out of 100. Score ties are resolved first by the higher objective score out of 90, then valid finish status and elapsed finish time, then fewer authorised resets, and then the earlier recorded time at the last common checkpoint (if available). Any unresolved seeding tie is settled by a recorded public draw. 

Wildcard allocations, seeded positions and any Advanced byes follow the announced selection and seeding procedures; institutions receive no special preference. Officials allocate lanes by draw and publish brackets before the relevant matches. The Organising Committee may revise the format only for documented operational or fairness reasons, with advance notice to affected teams.

## **2  Team and robot requirements** 

### **2.1  Eligibility and team responsibilities** 

**2.1.1** A team consists of one to three undergraduate students from the same university or higher-education institution. Each student may enter one team only. An institution may enter multiple teams. Officials may verify identity and student status. 

**2.1.2** Each team enters one robot, nominates a team leader and appoints one operator. Only that operator may start or reposition the robot. A change of operator requires referee permission. 

**2.1.3** Teams must assemble and program their own robots. Commercial components are permitted. Fully assembled ready-to-race robots and sharing a robot between entries are prohibited. 

### **2.2  Technical specifications** 

|**Item**|**Requirement**|
|---|---|
|Maximum footprint|20 cm wide × 20 cm long, including projecting components.|
|Height|No height limit. The robot must remain stable and safe.|
|Supply voltage|Maximum 24 V. Use onboard power.|
|Start control|Accessible physical switch or push button with a three-second stationary delay after<br>activation.|
|Navigation|Onboard sensors and controller. The robot must sense and follow the line.|
|External connections|No trailing cables, remote steering or external commands during a match.|
|Track contact|Wheels and other components must not stain, scratch, pierce, tear or contaminate the<br>track.|



### **2.3  Inspection and preparation** 

**2.3.1** Robots must pass inspection before competing. Officials may check dimensions, voltage, wiring, batteries, sensors, controls, wheels and autonomous operation. Unsecured batteries, hazardous wiring, leaking cells, sharp edges and track-damaging parts must be corrected before admission. 

**2.3.2** Officials may inspect code and request a demonstration when external control or a programmed shortcut is reasonably suspected. Route memorisation without line sensing is prohibited. 

**2.3.3** Be ready within five minutes of the official call. Calibrate sensors only in the designated starting area during the assigned preparation period. Teams must not reprogram, replace components or modify hardware during an official match. Changes between matches remain subject to inspection.

## **3  Basic round** 

The Basic round tests reliable line following, cornering and completion speed. Each match, including every wildcard match, has a three-minute time limit. 

### **3.1  Arena and course** 

The arena measures 10 ft × 12 ft, approximately 3.05 m × 3.66 m. It contains two separate, mirrored lanes. Each lane has its own start and finish and a nominal 15 mm black guide line on a flat white surface. Minor print seams may be present. 

The Basic course uses a continuous route with straight sections, bends and changes of direction. Each lane has six official checkpoint markers, worth 10 points each. The reference diagram shows a possible arrangement; final marker locations and traversal order are confirmed at the technical briefing. 





Figure 1  Illustrative Basic course, showing six checkpoints per mirrored lane. The final course is announced separately; this is not a scale construction drawing. 

### **3.2  Lane conditions** 

Both lanes must provide equivalent route length, difficulty, checkpoints, line quality and lighting. A divider or marked no-entry zone separates the lanes. Robots must operate under ordinary venue lighting and remain in their assigned lane. 

The course remains fixed within the round. Officials announce any repairs and apply them fairly to both lanes. Teams must follow the start direction, finish gate and checkpoint positions identified during the briefing. 

### **3.3  Qualification** 

Initial match winners advance under the announced qualification arrangements. Any wildcard matches use the same Basic course, 180-second limit, checkpoint scoring, time bonus and tie-breaking criteria. The published format governs wildcard selection and advancement.

## **4  Advanced round** 

Qualified teams compete in the Advanced knockout championship. Every Advanced match, including the final and third-place play-off, has a three-minute (180-second) time limit and the same 100-point scoring method. 

The Advanced course can include sharp turns, tight bends, broken-line sections, crossings and a white guide line through a black section. Robots must follow the intended route and reacquire the line after gaps. Both rounds use the arena and nominal line width specified in Section 3.1. Advanced matches use separate mirrored lanes of equivalent difficulty.

## **5  Race procedure** 

### **5.1  Start sequence** 

**1** Place the robot fully inside its assigned start zone, facing the required direction. Keep it stationary. Only nominated operators and accredited officials may enter the immediate competition zone. 

**2** At the referee’s activation signal, both operators press their start buttons. Each robot must remain stationary for three seconds after its button is pressed. Operators then step clear and send no further commands. 

**3** The referee gives the common GO signal at the end of the delay and starts the race clock. Robots depart autonomously. The three-second delay precedes race timing. A late departure uses race time. Movement before GO is a false start. 

The timing system records elapsed time to at least 0.1 seconds. Officials use manual stopwatches as backup. The central referee controls the start and each lane has separate supervision. 

### **5.2  Checkpoints** 

**5.2.1** Each round has six official checkpoints. A robot earns 10 points for each distinct checkpoint it crosses autonomously in the prescribed order, verified by the lane referee. Manual movement, shortcuts and out-oforder crossings earn no checkpoint credit. 

**5.2.2** A checkpoint can earn points only once per run, even if the robot passes it again after a reset. For example, three distinct verified checkpoints earn 30 points. Previously earned checkpoint marks are retained after an authorised reset. 

### **5.3  Recovery and authorised resets** 

**5.3.1** If a robot leaves the route, loses the line or becomes trapped, the operator must signal the lane referee and obtain permission before touching it. Autonomous recovery carries no penalty provided the robot remains on the required route and does not bypass checkpoints. 

**5.3.2** Only the nominated operator may reposition a robot, under referee supervision, at the exact marked location of its last verified checkpoint. If no checkpoint has been reached, return it to the start. Align the robot in the onward direction and release it without pushing or guiding it. 

**5.3.3** A maximum of six authorised repositionings is permitted per run. A seventh request ends the run as DNF. The clock continues throughout the request, handling and restart, including any repeat three-second start delay. No score deduction applies to an authorised repositioning; previously verified checkpoint points remain valid. 

### **5.4  Finish and time limit** 

A valid finish requires the robot to follow the assigned route, autonomously pass all six required checkpoints in order and cross its own finish gate before 180 seconds have elapsed. The referee records the valid finish time. A valid finish earns 10 points plus eligibility for the time bonus. 

A robot that fails to finish within 180 seconds or requests a seventh repositioning is recorded as did not finish (DNF). It keeps verified checkpoint marks and any applicable judges’ marks, but earns no finish-line points or time bonus. Stop or retrieve the robot only when directed by the referee.

## **6  Results and scoring** 

Each run is scored out of 100 points: 90 points for measurable track performance and 10 for a standardised technical assessment. The higher final score wins the match. Timing, checkpoints, judges’ marks and any penalties must be recorded independently and applied consistently in both rounds. 

|**Scoring component**|**Maximumpoints**|
|---|---|
|Six verified checkpoints(10 each)|60|
|Valid finish after all six checkpoints|10|
|Completion time bonus(valid finishers only)|20|
|Judges’ technical evaluation(three-judge average)|10|
|Unauthorised handling penalty|−5per incident|
|TOTAL AVAILABLE|100|



### **6.1  Judges’ technical evaluation** 

Three judges independently assess each robot using the same 10-point rubric. Their arithmetic mean, rounded to two decimal places, forms the judges’ score (maximum 10). Marks are submitted independently before total scores or rankings are disclosed. 

|**Technical criterion**|**Maximum**|**Observable assessment**|
|---|---|---|
|Line-following accuracy and<br>consistency|4|Maintains the guide line with limited overshoot or unnecessary<br>oscillation.|
|Stabilityand smooth movement|3|Moves consistentlywithout tipping,erratic motion or wheel instability.|
|Turns and challenging sections|3|Negotiates bends, gaps and direction changes unaided; autonomously<br>recovers when needed.|
|TOTAL|10|Applythe same criteria to everyteam,includingnon-finishers.|



Judges give full, partial or zero marks based on observed performance and the stated rubric. They record short reasons and sign their sheets. Institution, sponsor, cost, appearance and personal preference must not affect marks. The three-judge average, not the sum, is used in the 100-point score. 

### **6.2  Time bonus, final score and tie-breakers** 

Only valid finishers receive a time bonus. Time bonus = 20 × (180 − valid �inish time in seconds) ÷ 180, Only valid finishers receive a time bonus. Time bonus = 20 x (180 - valid finish time in seconds) + 180, rounded to two decimal places (minimum 0; maximum 20). For example, a 60-second finish earns 13.33 bonus points. DNF runs earn 0 time-bonus points. 

Final score = checkpoint points + finish points + time bonus + average judges’ marks − penalty points, with a minimum of 0. Ties are resolved in order by: (1) higher objective score out of 90 (excluding judges’ marks); (2) valid finish over DNF; (3) faster valid finish time; (4) fewer authorised repositionings; (5) earlier recorded time at the last common checkpoint. If still tied, the referee orders a documented supervised rematch.

## **7  Conduct and event information** 

### **7.1  Penalties** 

Remote steering, external commands, deliberate shortcuts, bypassed checkpoints, interference, track damage, and dishonest entry or result reporting are prohibited. Only the nominated operator may handle a robot after referee authorisation. Each distinct infringement and its official action must be recorded; authorised repositioning is not penalised twice. 

|**Infringement or event**|**Official action**|
|---|---|
|Leaving the track / approved repositioning|Return to the last verified checkpoint; timer continues. No point deduction. Up<br>to sixper run.|
|Seventh repositioningrequest|Run ends as DNF; previous checkpointpoints remain.|
|Unauthorised touching or handling|Deduct 5 points per incident; referee may order a reset. Deliberate<br>interference can lead to disqualification.|
|Missed checkpoint / unauthorised shortcut|No credit for the missed checkpoint. Finish and time bonus are invalid until all<br>checkpoints arepassed in order.|
|First accidental false start|Referee mayrestart the start sequence;this is not an in-race repositioning.|
|Repeated false start|Match forfeiture.|
|Exceeded 180-second limit|DNF; no finish-linepoints or time bonus.|
|External control, tamperingor dangerous conduct|Disqualification andpossible removal from the competition.|



Officials may stop a robot immediately if it presents a hazard. The head referee records the circumstances and remedy. A safety stop does not automatically grant a replay. One incident should not receive duplicate deductions for track departure and its authorised repositioning. 

### **7.2  Results review** 

The central referee verifies finish status, elapsed time and final score; lane referees record checkpoints, resets and rule infringements. Three judges sign their independent 10-point evaluations. Only the registered team leader may promptly request review of a factual or arithmetic recording error, before the next relevant bracket stage. 

A rematch may be ordered for verified timing failure, significant venue interference, materially unequal lane conditions or an unresolved scoring tie. The head referee and Organising Committee review the recorded evidence and issue the final decision under the published rules. Judges’ technical marks cannot be adjusted based on team identity or preferred outcomes. 

### **7.3  Awards and announcements** 

First place receives LKR 40,000. Second place receives LKR 30,000. The third-place play-off determines third place. Any additional awards are communicated separately by the organisers. 

The organisers issue the event date, venue, reporting time, match schedule and final bracket through official participant communications. Dated rule amendments must be communicated to all registered teams before they take effect. 

### **7.4  Before your match** 

Check your robot’s dimensions, voltage, battery security and three-second start delay. Complete inspection and attend the briefing. Confirm the six checkpoint positions, assigned lane, required route and finish gate. Be ready within five minutes of the call; only the nominated operator may perform an authorised reset (maximum six per run).
