IET ROBODASH 2026
INTERUNIVERSITY AUTONOMOUS LINE FOLLOWING COMPETITION RULEBOOK
Basic round and Advanced round

The rulebook sets out the entry requirements, robot specifications, race procedures and results criteria for all participating teams. Read both round descriptions before preparing a robot.

Participant edition

The competition includes Basic qualification and, if announced, wildcard matches, followed by the Advanced knockout championship.

Both rounds use the same published 100-point scoring system.

The reference Basic course has six checkpoint markers on each mirrored lane. Final layouts are confirmed at the technical briefing.

1. Competition format

Two teams race simultaneously on separate, mirrored lanes. Each robot follows the guide line autonomously.

The higher official 100-point score wins each match, subject to the published tie-breakers.

Qualification uses a knockout-based format, with wildcard opportunities where announced.

1.1 Round time limits

Basic round:
- Match time limit: 3 minutes or 180 seconds.
- Purpose: initial qualification and any wildcard matches.

Advanced round:
- Match time limit: 3 minutes or 180 seconds.
- Purpose: knockout championship, including placing matches.

1.2 Basic qualification

Teams compete in paired Basic matches.

Winners advance to Advanced under the published qualification arrangements.

Teams eliminated in the initial stage may be offered a wildcard opportunity, provided they have not been disqualified.

Wildcard selection considers official scores, valid completion times and the published tie-breakers.

The Organising Committee determines:
- Qualification places
- Wildcard places
- Match pairings
- Byes
- Advancement procedure

The selection is based on the total number of entrants. The competition bracket and advancement procedure are communicated before the competition begins.

Any necessary format change must be communicated to all affected teams before implementation.

1.3 Advanced championship

Opening elimination stage:
- Matches are based on the number of entrants.
- Pairings, places and byes are published.

Subsequent knockout stages:
- Matches are held as required.
- Winning teams advance according to the published bracket.

Quarter-finals:
- Are held if applicable.
- Winners advance to the semi-finals.

Semi-finals:
- Two matches are held.
- Winners enter the final.
- The other teams enter the third-place play-off.

Final and third-place play-off:
- Two matches determine first, second and third place.

1.4 Seeding and lane allocation

Officials seed teams using their official Basic scores out of 100.

The seeding tie-break order is:
1. Higher objective score out of 90.
2. Higher valid finish status.
3. Faster elapsed valid finish time.
4. Fewer authorised resets.
5. Earlier recorded time at the last common checkpoint, if available.

If the tie remains unresolved, it is settled by a recorded public draw.

Wildcard allocations, seeded positions and any Advanced byes follow the announced selection and seeding procedures.

Institutions receive no special preference.

Officials allocate lanes by draw and publish brackets before relevant matches.

The Organising Committee may revise the format only for documented operational or fairness reasons and must give advance notice to affected teams.

2. Team and robot requirements

2.1 Eligibility and team responsibilities

2.1.1
- A team consists of one to three undergraduate students.
- Team members must come from the same university or higher-education institution.
- Each student may enter only one team.
- An institution may enter multiple teams.
- Officials may verify identity and student status.

2.1.2
- Each team enters one robot.
- The team nominates one team leader.
- The team appoints one operator.
- Only that operator may start or reposition the robot.
- A change of operator requires referee permission.

2.1.3
- Teams must assemble and program their own robots.
- Commercial components are permitted.
- Fully assembled ready-to-race robots are prohibited.
- Sharing a robot between entries is prohibited.

2.2 Technical specifications

Maximum footprint:
- 20 cm wide.
- 20 cm long.
- Dimensions include projecting components.

Height:
- No height limit.
- The robot must remain stable and safe.

Power:
- Maximum supply voltage: 24 V.
- The robot must use onboard power.

Start control:
- The robot must have an accessible physical switch or push button.
- After activation, the robot must remain stationary for three seconds.

Navigation:
- The robot must use onboard sensors and a controller.
- It must sense and follow the line.

External connections:
- No trailing cables.
- No remote steering.
- No external commands during a match.

Track contact:
- Wheels and other components must not stain, scratch, pierce, tear or contaminate the track.

2.3 Inspection and preparation

2.3.1
- Robots must pass inspection before competing.
- Officials may check dimensions, voltage, wiring, batteries, sensors, controls, wheels and autonomous operation.
- Unsecured batteries, hazardous wiring, leaking cells, sharp edges and track-damaging parts must be corrected before admission.

2.3.2
- Officials may inspect code and request a demonstration when external control or a programmed shortcut is reasonably suspected.
- Route memorisation without line sensing is prohibited.

2.3.3
- Teams must be ready within five minutes of the official call.
- Sensors may be calibrated only in the designated starting area during the assigned preparation period.
- Teams must not reprogram, replace components or modify hardware during an official match.
- Changes between matches remain subject to inspection.

3. Basic round

The Basic round tests reliable line following, cornering and completion speed.

Each match, including every wildcard match, has a three-minute or 180-second limit.

3.1 Arena and course

- Arena size: 10 ft x 12 ft, approximately 3.05 m x 3.66 m.
- The arena contains two separate, mirrored lanes.
- Each lane has its own start and finish.
- The nominal guide line is 15 mm black on a flat white surface.
- Minor print seams may be present.

The Basic course has straight sections, bends and changes of direction.

Each lane has six official checkpoint markers, worth 10 points each.

The reference diagram shows a possible arrangement only.

Final marker locations and traversal order are confirmed at the technical briefing.

3.2 Lane conditions

- Both lanes must provide equivalent route length, difficulty, checkpoints, line quality and lighting.
- A divider or marked no-entry zone separates the lanes.
- Robots must operate under ordinary venue lighting.
- Robots must remain in their assigned lane.

The course remains fixed within the round.

Officials announce repairs and apply them fairly to both lanes.

Teams must follow the start direction, finish gate and checkpoint positions identified during the briefing.

3.3 Qualification

Initial match winners advance under the announced qualification arrangements.

Wildcard matches use:
- The same Basic course
- The 180-second limit
- Checkpoint scoring
- The time bonus
- The tie-breaking criteria

The published format governs wildcard selection and advancement.

4. Advanced round

Qualified teams compete in the Advanced knockout championship.

Every Advanced match, including the final and third-place play-off, has a three-minute or 180-second limit.

Every Advanced match uses the same 100-point scoring method.

The Advanced course may include:
- Sharp turns
- Tight bends
- Broken-line sections
- Crossings
- A white guide line through a black section

Robots must follow the intended route and reacquire the line after gaps.

Both rounds use the arena and nominal line width specified in Section 3.1.

Advanced matches use separate mirrored lanes of equivalent difficulty.

At the technical briefing, officials identify:
- The six checkpoint locations
- Required checkpoint order
- Final route
- Crossing directions
- Gaps
- Colour transitions

The labelled illustration alone does not define traversal order.

Robots must follow the announced route without taking shortcuts.

Checkpoint numbers and positions are verified on the final course.

The published layout and briefing govern the route.

Every rule amendment must be dated and issued to all teams before it takes effect.

5. Race procedure

5.1 Start sequence

Step 1:
- Place the robot fully inside its assigned start zone.
- Face the robot in the required direction.
- Keep it stationary.
- Only nominated operators and accredited officials may enter the immediate competition zone.

Step 2:
- At the referee's activation signal, both operators press their start buttons.
- Each robot must remain stationary for three seconds after activation.
- Operators then step clear.
- Operators send no further commands.

Step 3:
- The referee gives the common GO signal after the three-second delay.
- The referee starts the race clock.
- Robots depart autonomously.
- The three-second delay precedes race timing.
- A late departure uses race time.
- Movement before GO is a false start.

Timing:
- The timing system records elapsed time to at least 0.1 seconds.
- Officials use manual stopwatches as backup.
- The central referee controls the start.
- Each lane has separate supervision.

5.2 Checkpoints

5.2.1
- Each round has six official checkpoints.
- Each distinct checkpoint crossed autonomously in the prescribed order earns 10 points.
- The lane referee verifies crossings.
- Manual movement, shortcuts and out-of-order crossings earn no checkpoint credit.

5.2.2
- A checkpoint earns points only once per run.
- Passing the same checkpoint again after a reset does not earn it again.
- Previously earned checkpoint marks remain valid after an authorised reset.

Example:
- Three distinct verified checkpoints earn 30 points.

5.3 Recovery and authorised resets

5.3.1
- If a robot leaves the route, loses the line or becomes trapped, the operator must signal the lane referee.
- The operator must obtain permission before touching the robot.
- Autonomous recovery carries no penalty if the robot remains on the required route and does not bypass checkpoints.

5.3.2
- Only the nominated operator may reposition the robot.
- Repositioning is under referee supervision.
- The robot is returned to the exact marked location of its last verified checkpoint.
- If no checkpoint has been reached, return the robot to the start.
- Align the robot in the onward direction.
- Release it without pushing or guiding it.

5.3.3
- A maximum of six authorised repositionings is permitted per run.
- A seventh repositioning request ends the run as DNF.
- The clock continues throughout the request, handling and restart.
- This includes repeat three-second start delays.
- An authorised repositioning causes no score deduction.
- Previously verified checkpoint points remain valid.

5.4 Finish and time limit

A valid finish requires the robot to:
- Follow the assigned route.
- Autonomously pass all six required checkpoints in order.
- Cross its own finish gate before 180 seconds has elapsed.

The referee records the valid finish time.

A valid finish earns:
- 10 finish-line points.
- Eligibility for the time bonus.

A robot that:
- Does not finish within 180 seconds, or
- Requests a seventh repositioning

is recorded as DNF.

A DNF run:
- Keeps verified checkpoint marks.
- Keeps any applicable judges' marks.
- Earns no finish-line points.
- Earns no time bonus.

Stop or retrieve the robot only when directed by the referee.

6. Results and scoring

Each run is scored out of 100 points:
- 90 points for measurable track performance.
- 10 points for standardised technical assessment.

The higher final score wins the match.

Timing, checkpoints, judges' marks and penalties must be recorded independently and applied consistently in both rounds.

Scoring table:

Six verified checkpoints:
- Maximum 60 points.
- 10 points per checkpoint.

Valid finish after all six checkpoints:
- Maximum 10 points.

Completion time bonus:
- Maximum 20 points.
- Valid finishers only.

Judges' technical evaluation:
- Maximum 10 points.
- Three-judge average.

Unauthorised handling penalty:
- Minus 5 points per incident.

Maximum available score:
- 100 points.

Minimum final score:
- 0 points.

6.1 Judges' technical evaluation

Three judges independently assess each robot using the same 10-point rubric.

Their arithmetic mean, rounded to two decimal places, forms the judges' score, with a maximum of 10.

Marks are submitted independently before total scores or rankings are disclosed.

Technical rubric:

Line-following accuracy and consistency:
- Maximum 4 points.
- The robot maintains the guide line with limited overshoot or unnecessary oscillation.

Stability and smooth movement:
- Maximum 3 points.
- The robot moves consistently without tipping, erratic motion or wheel instability.

Turns and challenging sections:
- Maximum 3 points.
- The robot negotiates bends, gaps and direction changes unaided.
- It recovers autonomously when needed.

Total:
- Maximum 10 points.

The same criteria apply to every team, including non-finishers.

Judges give full, partial or zero marks based on observed performance and the stated rubric.

Judges record short reasons and sign their sheets.

Institution, sponsor, cost, appearance and personal preference must not affect marks.

The three-judge average, not the sum, is used in the 100-point score.

6.2 Time bonus

Only valid finishers receive a time bonus.

Time bonus formula:

20 x (180 minus valid finish time in seconds) divided by 180

Round the result to two decimal places.

Minimum time bonus:
- 0 points.

Maximum time bonus:
- 20 points.

Example:
- A 60-second finish earns 13.33 bonus points.

DNF runs earn:
- 0 time-bonus points.

6.3 Final score

Final score =
checkpoint points
+ finish points
+ time bonus
+ average judges' marks
- penalty points

The minimum final score is 0.

6.4 Tie-breakers

Ties are resolved in this order:
1. Higher objective score out of 90, excluding judges' marks.
2. Valid finish over DNF.
3. Faster valid finish time.
4. Fewer authorised repositionings.
5. Earlier recorded time at the last common checkpoint.

If the tie is still unresolved, the referee orders a documented supervised rematch.

7. Conduct and event information

7.1 Penalties

The following conduct is prohibited:
- Remote steering
- External commands
- Deliberate shortcuts
- Bypassed checkpoints
- Interference
- Track damage
- Dishonest entry or result reporting
- External control
- Tampering
- Dangerous conduct

Only the nominated operator may handle a robot after referee authorisation.

Each distinct infringement and its official action must be recorded.

Authorised repositioning is not penalised twice.

Penalty table:

Leaving the track or approved repositioning:
- Return to the last verified checkpoint.
- The timer continues.
- No point deduction.
- Up to six repositionings per run.

Seventh repositioning request:
- The run ends as DNF.
- Previous checkpoint points remain.

Unauthorised touching or handling:
- Deduct 5 points per incident.
- The referee may order a reset.
- Deliberate interference can lead to disqualification.

Missed checkpoint or unauthorised shortcut:
- No credit for the missed checkpoint.
- Finish and time bonus are invalid until all checkpoints are passed in the required order.

First accidental false start:
- The referee may restart the start sequence.
- This is not an in-race repositioning.

Repeated false start:
- Match forfeiture.

Exceeded 180-second limit:
- DNF.
- No finish-line points.
- No time bonus.

External control, tampering or dangerous conduct:
- Disqualification.
- Possible removal from the competition.

Officials may stop a robot immediately if it presents a hazard.

The head referee records:
- The circumstances
- The remedy

A safety stop does not automatically grant a replay.

One incident must not receive duplicate deductions for track departure and its authorised repositioning.

7.2 Results review

The central referee verifies:
- Finish status
- Elapsed time
- Final score

Lane referees record:
- Checkpoints
- Resets
- Rule infringements

Three judges sign their independent 10-point evaluations.

Only the registered team leader may promptly request review of a factual or arithmetic recording error.

The review request must occur before the next relevant bracket stage.

A rematch may be ordered for:
- Verified timing failure
- Significant venue interference
- Materially unequal lane conditions
- An unresolved scoring tie

The head referee and Organising Committee review the recorded evidence.

They issue the final decision under the published rules.

Judges' technical marks cannot be adjusted based on team identity or preferred outcomes.

7.3 Awards and announcements

First place:
- LKR 40,000.

Second place:
- LKR 30,000.

Third place:
- Determined by the third-place play-off.

No additional awards are confirmed by this rulebook.
Additional awards must be communicated separately by the organisers.

The organisers issue the following through official participant communications:
- Event date
- Venue
- Reporting time
- Match schedule
- Final bracket

Dated rule amendments must be communicated to all registered teams before taking effect.

7.4 Before your match

Before the match:
- Check robot dimensions.
- Check robot voltage.
- Check battery security.
- Check the three-second start delay.
- Complete inspection.
- Attend the technical briefing.
- Confirm the six checkpoint positions.
- Confirm the assigned lane.
- Confirm the required route.
- Confirm the finish gate.
- Be ready within five minutes of the official call.

Only the nominated operator may perform an authorised reset.

Maximum authorised resets:
- Six per run.
